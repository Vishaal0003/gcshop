# 🤑 GCStore — The "Too Good To Be True" Store

> ⚠️ **Cybersecurity Awareness Project — Made for Learning & Testing Only**

Welcome to **GCStore** — an intentionally fake online gift-card/e-commerce store built to show **how easily a convincing phishing website can fool someone**.

Basically, the whole idea is:

**"Yo, look at this crazy discount 👀"**

User sees the offer → gets interested → starts shopping → reaches checkout → enters the payment flow → boom 💀

That's where the awareness lesson kicks in.

---

## 🧠 So... What Is GCStore?

GCStore is a **phishing-awareness simulation** designed to look like a normal online shopping website.

The website uses the same kind of stuff real scam websites use:

- 🔥 Crazy-low prices
- 🎁 Attractive gift-card deals
- 🛒 E-commerce-style product pages
- 💳 Fake checkout/payment flow
- ⏰ Urgency / limited-time offers
- ⭐ Trust-looking UI elements
- 🧠 Social-engineering tricks

The goal isn't to actually steal anything.

The goal is to show:

> **"If a website looks legit, that doesn't mean it IS legit."**

---

# 🎣 How The Trap Works

Here's the basic flow:

```text
👀 See Crazy Offer
       ↓
😍 "Yo, that's cheap!"
       ↓
🛒 Browse Products
       ↓
🎁 Pick Gift Card
       ↓
💳 Go To Checkout
       ↓
😬 Enter Payment Details
       ↓
💀 Fake Transaction
       ↓
🎟️ Fake Voucher / Card
       ↓
🧠 Awareness Lesson
```

And yeah... that's basically how social engineering works.

The attacker doesn't necessarily need some crazy 0-day exploit.

Sometimes they just need to make you think:

> **"Bro, this deal is WAY too good to pass up."**

---

# 😂 Why Make It Look Legit?

Because that's the whole point.

If the website looked like:

```text
WELCOME TO TOTALLY-SCAM.COM
GIVE US YOUR CARD 💳😂
```

nobody would fall for it.

Real phishing pages usually try to look **normal**.

They may copy the general design patterns people already trust:

- Online stores
- Banking pages
- Login portals
- Delivery websites
- Gift-card stores
- Payment pages

GCStore demonstrates that **visual trust ≠ actual trust**.

---

# 💳 The Fake Payment Part

When the user reaches checkout, the website demonstrates how a scammer might try to convince them to submit sensitive information.

For this project, everything should remain **simulated**.

No real:

- 💳 Card numbers
- 🔐 Passwords
- 🔢 CVV/CVC
- 💰 Payments
- 🏦 Banking information

should be collected or stored.

Use fake/test data only.

The point is to recreate the **experience**, not actually hurt anybody.

---

# 🎁 And Then... The "Gift Card"

After the simulated checkout, the user gets a fake voucher/card result.

At this point the user might think:

> "Aight, I actually got the card 😎"

But the whole thing is part of the simulation.

This demonstrates another social-engineering trick:

**Give the victim something that looks like confirmation so they believe everything was legitimate.**

# 🚩 Things Users Should Have Noticed

The fun part is figuring out what should've raised the red flags 🚩

Users should check for:

### 💸 1. Insane Discounts

If a ₹10,000 gift card is suddenly being sold for ₹999...

Maybe don't start celebrating yet 💀

### 🌐 2. Weird Domain

Always check the actual domain.

Scammers love domains that look *almost* right.

### 🔒 3. HTTPS Doesn't Mean "Safe"

That little 🔒 doesn't magically make a website trustworthy.

HTTPS protects the connection.

It does **not** prove the website isn't a scam.

### ⏰ 4. Fake Urgency

Stuff like:

> "ONLY 2 LEFT!"

> "SALE ENDS IN 03:21!"

> "LAST CHANCE!!!"

That's classic pressure tactics.

### 💳 5. Suspicious Payment Requests

If a random website starts asking for way more financial information than it reasonably needs...

**Pause.**

### 🏢 6. No Real Company Information

Look for:

- Company details
- Contact information
- Refund policy
- Terms
- Privacy policy
- Legitimate support channels

And verify them independently.

# 🧪 Why I Built This

I built GCStore to understand phishing from the **victim's point of view**.

Instead of just reading:

> "Phishing uses social engineering."

I wanted to actually understand the journey:

```text
Trust
 ↓
Curiosity
 ↓
Excitement
 ↓
Commitment
 ↓
Payment
 ↓
Potential Loss
```

That's what makes phishing dangerous.

It's not always about complicated hacking.

Sometimes it's just **good psychology + a convincing website**.

# 🎯 The Main Lesson

The biggest lesson from GCStore is simple:

> **Don't trust a website just because it looks professional.**

That clean UI?

Can be fake.

That crazy discount?

Could be bait.

That "limited time" message?

Could be pressure.

That payment page?

Could be the trap.

So before you enter sensitive information:

**Stop → Check → Verify → Then proceed.**

Because sometimes...

> **The biggest vulnerability isn't the server.**

> **It's the person sitting behind the keyboard. 😭💀**
